L21: KVarN is a KV cache quantizer from Huawei, published as cite18†KVarN: Variance-Normalized KV-Cache Quantization Mitigates Error Accumulation in Reasoning Tasks†arxiv.org (arXiv 2606.03458), with a reference implementation released as a cite19†vLLM fork†github.com . Its starting observation is about how KV quantizers are usually judged. Most are measured on prefill-shaped work, where a whole context is scored in one pass, while a reasoning model spends most of its time generating one token at a time.
L22: The paper argues that quantization error behaves differently in that second regime, building up across timesteps, and that the buildup is driven mainly by badly estimated per-token scales.
L23: The proposed fix needs no calibration data and has two stages. A Hadamard rotation is applied per head to K and V after RoPE. Rotating mixes every channel of a head into every other, so a single outlier channel no longer stretches the quantization range for the whole head. The query is rotated by the same matrix, which leaves the QK product unchanged, so attention can run in the rotated frame and the result is rotated back once per token rather than per stored token.
L24: A variance normalization then rescales both axes of each matrix before rounding, which is the stage aimed at the token-scale errors directly. The method runs that normalization as 16 Sinkhorn iterations. On the paper's own benchmarks the combination sets a two-bit state of the art on MATH500, AIME24, and HumanEval.
L25: The subject here is cite20†BeeLlama†github.com 's implementation of that method rather than the reference, and the measurement is teacher-forced Wikitext KLD rather than the reasoning benchmarks the paper reports. Those are different questions, and a result in one does not transfer to the other.
L26: ## 2. What KVarN Stores
L27: ### 2.1 Sink, Body, And Tail
L28: KVarN is a structured K/V cache format rather than another standard block type, and its precision depends on where a token sits in the sequence. The first 128 tokens stay exact permanently, which preserves the attention-sink behavior models rely on. Everything after that is quantized in groups of 128 tokens, and a group is quantized at the moment its last token arrives. Each group is rotated and normalized before quantization, and the layout stores the quantized payload with its scale metadata.
L29: Attention paths that support the format read these records directly; others convert to a standard cache layout first.
L30: The newest tokens have not filled a group yet, so they stay exact until they do. That trailing exact span is what this article calls the tail: a run of the most recent tokens held at higher precision than the rest of the cache. A standard cache treats every token alike, so a tail there is a feature you switch on. In KVarN it is a consequence of the group size, and it is never entirely absent.
L31: In the published design the tail is nothing more than that partial group. It grows from empty toward the group size and collapses each time a group seals, so the number of exact recent tokens is whatever has arrived since the last flush. The paper names the region R and sets it to 128 alongside a group size of 128, and the reference implementation sizes its F16 side buffer for two blocks per request per layer: the permanent sink and the one in-progress tail.
L32: ### 2.2 What This Implementation Changes
L33: BeeLlama does not let the region shrink. It floors the effective tail at one full group and then routes KVarN through the same precision-tail machinery that serves standard caches, so the cache holds at least 128 recent tokens exact rather than however many happen to have arrived. That floor is why a request of `--kv-tail-tokens 0` resolves to 128, and it is what makes a stated suffix length comparable between the two families at all.
L34: It is a deviation from the reference, and it moves in KVarN's favor, so the rows below describe this arrangement rather than the published one.
L35: Two further details matter for the comparison. This implementation keeps the prefix sink on non-sliding-window layers only; sliding-window layers have no permanent sink, though they still carry their local exact suffix. And the matched tables hold suffix length constant while other properties still differ: standard tails use BF16 in these runs, KVarN tails use F16, and non-SWA KVarN carries the prefix sink.
L36: ## 3. Matching The Baseline
L37: 
L38: Because KVarN always carries that exact suffix, a comparison against a tail-free standard cache does not result in an honest measurement of quantizers. Wikitext KLD scores the next token at every position, so an exact recent region is worth a great deal to it, and the standard cache can be given the same region for a known price.

L41: `q2_0`  | 0.019374  | 0.007521  | −61.2%
L42: `q4_0`  | 0.001846  | 0.001206  | −34.7%

L44: No quantizer changed between those columns; the cache simply stopped compressing its newest 128 tokens. None of it is a claim about output. Only 128 tokens of a 65536-token cache change hands, and scoring every position rewards exact recent context far more heavily than most real work does.
L45: The tail sweep is its own subject, covered in the cite21†precision-tail article ; here it exists only to make the two families comparable.
L46: That gradient is why an unmatched comparison misleads worst exactly where KVarN is most interesting. Place a KVarN two-bit row beside a tail-free standard two-bit row and most of the visible gap is the suffix, not the format. At eight bits the same confound is worth under 1% on either model.
L47: So every comparison from here on holds the suffix constant, which gives three published row types. Standard tail 0 is the diagnostic control above. Standard tail 128 is the matched comparator. KVarN tail 128* is the deployable configuration measured against it, and it has no tail 0 counterpart because that configuration can not exist.

L79: ## 5. Changing Only The Format
L80: Section 4 compares configurations of different sizes, which leaves room to read KVarN's position as bought with memory rather than earned by the format. The same-width rows close that off, because they change one variable. At four bits Qwen `kvarn4` records a median KLD of 0.001111 against `q4_0`'s 0.001206 while its quantized body is the smaller of the two, 1136.00 MiB against 1152.00. The 24.00 MiB that makes the KVarN total larger is exact overlay, which neither configuration compresses.
L81: Whatever produces the difference is happening inside a body with fewer bytes in it.

L92: The same-width column is a reading taken on ground that tilts away from the thing being measured.

Read these percentages as what this measurement can see, not as the true difference between the formats.

L111: ## 7. Gemma Changes The Tradeoff
L112: 
L113: Gemma behaves differently. Its 50 sliding-window layers and ten full-attention layers create a discontinuity when an exact suffix covers the visible 1024-token window, and even at 128 tokens the accounting has already diverged from Qwen's. Standard `q4_0` uses 808.36 MiB at median KLD 0.080066, while `kvarn4` tail 128* uses 1008.98 MiB at 0.058185: a 27.3% lower median at four bits, against 7.9% on Qwen, for 200.62 MiB more.

L122: That 200.62 MiB is fixed across all six widths, as the 24.00 MiB is on Qwen, but it is charged somewhere else. Prefill is more expensive too, falling from 6.9% at two bits to 14.0% at eight against 5.1% to 6.7% on Qwen.
L123: Longer tails change the memory arithmetic again, which is why the practical comparison belongs with the cite21†full tail sweep rather than here.
L124: Two bits reverses the outcome outright. At the matched suffix `kvarn2` tail 128* records 2.091697 median KLD against 1.329847 for standard `q2_0`, 57.3% worse for 200.62 MiB more. This is the only symmetric width in either model where a KVarN row records a higher median KLD than its same-width standard counterpart at the same published tail, and on Gemma the advantage only starts at three bits.
L125: This is why the recommendation cannot be copied between models. Qwen offers a smooth body-width ladder for a near-fixed 24.00 MiB and about 6% of its prefill throughput. Gemma charges 200.62 MiB and as much as 14.0% of throughput for the same move, so a standard high-bit body can remain the better default there even where the compressed row records the lower median.
L126: ## 8. What This Does Not Prove
L127: 
L128: This is one teacher-forced Wikitext-2 pass per configuration, not a coding-agent evaluation. It does not measure retrieval of an old symbol, adherence to repository instructions, correct tool selection, valid JSON, edit quality, or accumulated autoregressive error. KLD, perplexity, same-top probability, prefill throughput, and decode throughput are related observations, not interchangeable quality scores.
L129: Every row here holds its newest 128 tokens exact, so none of them says much about a decision made tens of thousands of tokens earlier. The KVarN prefix sink, the tail floor this implementation adds on top of the published design, F16 versus BF16 tail representation, one GPU, and the unrecovered binary hash of the quality runs are further limits. Close high-bit rows are best treated as a band.
L130: ## 9. Method
L131: The quality matrix uses one RTX 3090 on Windows 11, cite20†BeeLlama v0.4.0†github.com , CUDA 13.1 build. The two models are Qwen 3.6 27B Q5_K_S at 65536 context and Gemma 4 31B Q5_K_S at 16384 context. Both use Wikitext-2 raw, batch size 2048, micro-batch size 512, seed 1, FlashAttention, unified KV, no mmap, mlock, and no host offload. Every row is measured against its model's BF16-cache baseline.
L132: The complete standalone view contains BF16, all 48 standard pairs at tails 0 and 128, and all 15 selected KVarN pairs at tail 128* for each model.
L133: Median KLD ranks the typical scored position, and the mean and upper percentiles show whether a similar median hides a heavier error tail. Same-top probability asks whether the most likely token still agrees with the BF16-cache baseline. Tok/s is prompt processing measured inside the run, not generation speed.
L134: KV cache cells show the persistent total, reconstructed from each selected run's runtime buffer accounting. Where a cache pair also has a tail 0 row, a smaller `(Δ...)` gives the difference from that tail 0 total. KVarN tail 128* is its own requested-0 baseline and carries no delta, because its exact overlay is already inside the total.
