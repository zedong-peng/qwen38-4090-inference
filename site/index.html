Qwen3.8 27B on an RTX 4090. Artificial Analysis times how long a computer takes to serve a recorded coding agent. A 2022 gaming card finishes in nine minutes, behind only the RTX 5090.

Memory decides whether it runs, bandwidth how fast

Each step of writing reads all 16 GB of weights, so memory bandwidth sorts the table into tiers: the RTX 5090 at 1,792 GB/s, the RTX 4090 at 1,008, and the rest at 256 to 307, with room for far larger models but slow to read them.

Twenty-four gigabytes is just enough. With llama.cpp’s weights loaded, the 65,536-token cache still fits. vLLM’s 4-bit checkpoint does not: it keeps the embedding and output layers at 16 bits, loads 17.6 GiB, and leaves 2.35 GiB for a cache that needs 2.91.

An agent reads far more than it writes

Over 168 turns the agent sends prompt tokens and gets back . Most of each prompt was already sent the turn before and sits in the cache; the new tokens still take nearly four minutes to read. Writing takes the rest.

Speculative decoding shortens only the writing. DFlash drafts seven tokens at a time where MTP drafts two, and writes . It saves nearly two minutes of writing and none of the reading.
